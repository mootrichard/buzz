# BizBuySell listing 2524202: likely identity

Research date: 2026-07-30

## Conclusion

The listing is almost certainly **R3 Engraving & Signs**, operated by
**R3 AND ME, LLC**, in Corvallis, Oregon.

Confidence: **about 99%**. This is an evidence-backed inference, not a
confirmation from the seller or broker.

The phrase match is the decisive fingerprint. The HP and OSU work shown in
R3’s own portfolio independently corroborates the listing’s customer language.
No separate Corvallis candidate found has a comparable combination of the exact
three-R phrase, 1962 history, and named HP/OSU work.

The 1962 date describes the operating business and brand history. The current

present legal entity and determine whether the transaction is an asset or
equity sale.

## What the business actually does

This appears to be a small, owner-led custom fabrication shop with several
revenue streams:

- Industrial tags, component labels, equipment markings, and legend plates
- ADA, room, directory, exterior, real-estate, and wayfinding signs
- Large-format digital printing, vinyl lettering, and vehicle graphics
- Name badges, plaques, trophies, awards, donor recognition, and memorials
- Laser, rotary, diamond, hand, and sand-carved engraving

The listing’s $60,000 inventory likely consists largely of substrates, sign and
engraving stock, plaque/award blanks, metals, plastics, vinyl, adhesives, and
promotional-product components. That composition must be verified from the
inventory schedule.

## Listing economics

- Asking price: $475,000
- Revenue: $760,000
- SDE: $140,000
- SDE margin: 18.4%
- Asking price / revenue: 0.63x
- Asking price / SDE: 3.39x
- Annual rent: $54,000, or 7.1% of reported revenue
- Inventory included: $60,000

The 3.39x SDE multiple is only meaningful if the owner’s hours and add-backs are
understood. If a buyer must hire a manager or replace the seller’s design,
estimating, customer, or finance work, normalized earnings could be materially
below the advertised SDE.

## Highest-priority diligence

1. Obtain three to five years of revenue and gross margin by customer, especially
   HP and OSU. Determine whether work is contractual, purchase-order based, or
   simply repeat business.
2. Rebuild SDE from tax returns, general ledgers, payroll, and owner add-backs.
   Price a replacement for every recurring seller responsibility.
3. Get the full equipment list with ownership status, serial numbers, age,
   maintenance history, remaining useful life, and replacement cost.
4. Count and age the $60,000 inventory. Exclude obsolete, customer-specific, or
   slow-moving stock.
5. Review the lease term, assignment rights, renewal options, rent escalations,
   permitted use, and whether all fabrication processes are allowed.
6. Confirm that the trade name, domain, phone number, design files, customer
   records, vendor accounts, software, licenses, and warranties transfer.
7. Check whether public-sector or construction work depends on contractor or
   ownership certifications that a buyer would need to replace.
